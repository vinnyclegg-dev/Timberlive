# Lessons for the next playthrough (Iron Teeth, map "d")
Rules learned the hard way in playthrough 1. Read with `07-opening-plan-v2.md` at the start of a new game.

## Food
- FarmHouse defaults to planting first, so ripe crops rot unharvested. Set `UnprioritizePlanting` straight away (watch.py does it).
- One FarmHouse (2 workers) tends roughly 50-60 plots. Plan 1 FarmHouse per ~55 plots, inside about 11 tiles.
- Kohlrabi takes about 3.3 days to grow and slows in droughts. Plant fields a full cycle before the food is needed, and harvest into storage before each hazard.
- Bushes can sit at 0 ripe for days, so berries are not a reliable buffer early on.

## Water and hazards
- The river dries completely by day 2-3 of a drought, even with dams. Stored water is what counts.
- Reserve rule: target = 1.5 x the last hazard's use per beaver per day (~1.6 water, ~1.8 food) x **projected** beavers (with pod births) x the hazard's days. Always scale by population, never compare raw totals.
- Breeding Pods eat a lot of water and berries. Before a hazard, if stock is falling, pause half the pods until the trend turns.
- Build storage ahead: 1 SmallTank (15 logs) per ~2 beavers before drought 3.

## Map and paths
- Map "d" is multi-level. Builders can't reach a lower level (the riverbed, dams) without Stairs (1 log + 4 planks). Unlock Stairs early.
- Don't box the base in. Leave a road corridor out of the core in every direction before filling gaps with fields, tanks or bushes.
- Crops block path placement. Mark them with `demolish_resources` (builders remove them over time), or better, don't plant on future corridors.
- DeepWaterPump pipes take the tile in front, one level down. Never path there.
- Scout the map's natural objects on day 1 with `map_summary(section="features")` and `section="links"`. The first playthrough missed the natural Slopes (free stairs) and built Stairs instead. Also note Natural Dams, Blockages, natural Overhangs, Relics (science), Thorns, ruins, Reserve stores and the Unstable Core (10 §16, 11 A5).

## Wood (the real bottleneck)
- Logs ran out several times: one stand plus one Forester isn't enough past ~12 beavers.
- Build a second Forester and 4+ lumberjacks by about cycle 3, and mark every grown stand early (oaks across the river).
- A CompactWaterWheel stops in droughts, so the mill can't make planks then. Stock planks before each drought.

## Planning ahead
- Grow production **before** the demand: logs, then planks, then gears (GearWorkshop at 100 science) unlocks MediumTank, the Weather Station and automation.
- Each new barrack needs 40 logs. Add housing one pod-cycle before the beds run out.

## Wood, continued (cycle 4)
- Forester + lumberjack flags: flags are free (no cost). Forester = 10 logs + 7 planks. Run 2 Foresters by cycle 3 at the latest.
- Dead/dried trees still yield logs. Mark old dead stands (the plateau had 112 dead pines) for cutting before planting new ones.
- Logs per tile-day: Oak 0.27 > Pine 0.17 > Birch 0.14. Pine/birch for quick wood, oak on outer rows for long-term wood.
- Clearing bushes, crops and trees costs builder time (`demolish_resources`, the toolbar's Demolish resources tool). For trees, prefer a cutting area so the logs aren't lost. Plan corridors before planting.
- Natural Slope tiles connect levels for free (e.g. (59,37) h2 to h3), so check for slopes before spending planks on Stairs.
- Build river crossings (Stairs into the bed) during a drought, while the bed is dry.
- Planks have **no storage** until a warehouse is set to Plank. The mill stops when its own 13-slot output is full. Build a Plank warehouse together with the mill.
- Iron Teeth have the LargePowerWheel (50 logs, beaver-powered). It works in droughts, unlike water wheels, so use it for industry.

## Storage
- Always build storage for a resource before or with its producer, and add more before it fills. watch.py prints `STORAGE FULL` for goods at 95% or more.
- Logs and planks go in **Industrial Piles** (Small = 4 logs, 1x1). SmallWarehouses accept the Plank setting but never receive planks.
- `demolish_resources` also clears the planting marks in the area (like the toolbar tool), so planters don't regrow the tiles.
- Put a 1x1 store for each workshop's input right beside its door, set to that good, and set it to **obtain** (`GoodObtainer EnableObtaining`). Keep big long-term stores elsewhere, set to **supply** (`GoodSupplier EnableSupplying`). Add a Hauling Post near industry clusters. Example: Plank pile (68,23) obtaining next to the GearWorkshop door (69,23), plus a Gear pile at (68,22).
- Count workers before building industry. The LargePowerWheel needs 4 workers and sat idle with 0 free adults, so time power and workshops to match kits growing up.
- Obtaining stores show "Requires haulers to function" until a Hauling Post exists. Build the Hauling Post (20 logs, 10 planks, 5-10 workers) before relying on obtain/supply. Hauling Post ori2 at (72,29) z4 has its door at (71,30).
- Match workers to demand. The LargePowerWheel gives ~75 hp per worker (300 with 4); the GearWorkshop needs 120, so 2 workers are enough. Use `set_workers` on multi-worker buildings (Hauling Post, power wheel, farms) and spend the freed beavers elsewhere.
- Use both priorities: construction priority on sites, and work priority on finished buildings, so the important jobs (gears, food, water) get staffed first when workers are short.
- Don't power with beavers. Build industry by the river, with Compact Water Wheels (120 hp each, no workers) linked by `PowerShaft.IronTeeth`. Network this game: wheel (71,52) (links to the mill at its middle (72,51)) + wheel (74,53), shafts (75,51),(75,52), mill (71,49) 75 hp, GearWorkshop (73,49) 120 hp, door (74,48) on path y48.
- Reserve sizing: use the LONGEST hazard so far (not the last one), scaled to the projected beaver count.
- Power links: water wheels connect only at the **middle** of their long side, at mid height (a 3x1 Compact Wheel at x74-76 y53 links at (75,52) or (75,54)). Buildings that use power pass it on to neighbours they touch. Use Vertical Power Shafts (needs science) to take power up or down a level.
- Wellbeing = lifespan + work speed + movement (tiers: work +20%/+40% at 5/10, lifespan +20% at 7). Add cheap +1s early: Campfire, Shrubs (area effect, no path needed), food variety (Cassava + Fermenter). Shrubs this game: (65,41),(70,41); cassava patch x80-86 y54-55.
- Plants don't grow in water unless their description says so. Never mark planting on MOISTURE `w` tiles. Check with the scratchpad `wetplants.py`-style scan, then `clear_planting` on any water tiles.
- Rooftop Terrace sits on a roof, and beavers need **Platforms** (or stairs) to reach it. Its door was at z6 above barrack (70,35).
- Some structures can be walked on, e.g. **dams** (path on top). Use them to cross rivers and channels instead of building stairs and paths around.
- Lay roads **before** planting fields or forests. Leave a corridor about every 8-10 tiles and never plant on it. This game: road y48 x67-94 and spur x88 y48-57 cut through the kohlrabi/pine block into the green riverbank land.
- Only use what a player has on the toolbar or building panels. No dev-mode tools (the old instant `remove_plants` was one and is gone).
- Oak is the most efficient tree for wood overall (8 logs, about 0.27 logs per tile-day). Set every Forester to prioritise Oak, and use birch or pine only for quick early wood (and pine for resin).
- Don't pause a support building (e.g. Hauling Post) just to save workers. Give it Low job priority (set_priority on a finished building = work priority) so it takes only spare beavers.
- Drought: once the tanks hold the reserve, pause most water pumps. Pumping drains the river and dries the crop fields. Keep one pump running.
- During a hazard, raise food jobs (FarmHouse, GathererFlag) to High work priority, and keep support jobs (Hauling Post, Inventor) Low.
- Berry storage fills fast (gatherers stop when it's full). Keep about 2 days of berry storage per gatherer, and add a SmallWarehouse for Berries before the cap is reached.
- Record every lesson here straight away (the user's rule), so the next playthrough starts better.
- Before replacing or removing a path tile, check that no building's door (`pathTile`) uses it. I put stairs on the MediumTank's door tile (76,34) and cut the tank off, losing 300 water capacity mid-drought. Check with `[b for b in buildings() if b.get('pathTile')[:2]==[x,y]]`.
- Rotate the camera and look (`set_camera` horizontal_angle, then one screenshot) before and after changing paths, stairs or buildings in a tight spot. A single camera angle hides doors and blocked tiles behind buildings.
- Drought 5 (8 days, up from 5) dried out every field. All the kohlrabi died, and bushes, cassava and trees were dying by day 6. Hazards get longer each cycle, so:
  - size the reserve on the longest hazard plus one more cycle of growth (5 -> 8 days here)
  - keep farms on land fed by a dam reservoir or irrigation, not on riverbank that dries out
  - stock food in warehouses, because crops in the ground are not a reserve
- Breeding Pods eat berries. Pause them as soon as the food forecast shows stock running out before the hazard ends plus the regrow time.
- Idle beavers: raise worker counts (set_workers) where there is room (District Center builders, Hauling Post), and use job priority to pick who gets them first (food High, builders High while key sites wait, haulers and inventors Low). Don't leave beavers unemployed.

## Cycle 6 (after drought 5)
- **Count only edible food.** Raw Cassava, Soybean, Corn, Eggplant and Potato are ingredients, not food; they need a Fermenter / Food Factory / Grill first. The overlay and watch.py counted raw Cassava, so "food 15" was really 0. Both now count edible goods only. Build the Fermenter in the same step as the first cassava field.
- **Starvation cost 17 of 30 beavers** in about 4 days after drought 5. Fields died in the drought, the replant needs 3 days, and the only big ripe berry patches (x96-127, y64-95) are far off on high ground with no road. Next time: food reserve in storage before the hazard (see goals.md), a gatherer road to the far bushes in a calm season, and farms beside dam water.
- **In a food crisis, move hands to food:** drop idle builders (DistrictCenter workers 4 -> 2 when there are no sites), set the full lumber mill to VeryLow, put one pump at Low and the empty FarmHouse at VeryHigh.
- **Search for spots quietly:** `place_building(..., check=True)` tests a spot like hovering the preview. It places nothing and doesn't move the camera. Never brute-force real placements: every call pans the camera, and fast back-and-forth panning makes viewers motion sick.
- **Camera tour:** one slow pan every 45 s, to the nearest site or busy workplace not yet shown.
- **Population crash: pause, don't just reprioritise.** Normally use job priority. When beavers are starving or there are far more job slots than beavers, pause every workplace that isn't food or water (lumberjacks, foresters, inventors, hauling post, mill, spare pumps). Unpause them one by one as the population recovers.
- **Camera that entertains:** always something to watch; a still camera is fine while there is action in view, but never parked on an idle spot. don't rotate it constantly (the user disliked a non-stop orbit). watch.py pans (a smooth 2.5 s glide) every 30 s to the nearest working beaver (`working_beavers()`) or building site.
- **Layers and work hours:** `set_layers(level)` hides everything above a height, like the player's layer slider, to check tunnels, intakes and lower floors (`set_layers()` resets it). `set_work_hours` lengthens shifts for a push and shortens them when sleep or wellbeing is low.
- **Pods are cheap:** a Breeding Pod uses only 1 Berry + 1 Water per day (5 days per kit). After a crash, restart them as soon as there are a few days of berries. They are the only way IT grow, and lost time means fewer workers later.
- **Drought 6 (c7):** pausing the pumps wasn't enough. By the end of a 6-day drought the river by the fields was completely dry and every crop was dying (kohlrabi at 42%, cassava, bushes). The river only holds water through a drought if dams/levees make a deep reservoir upstream of the fields. Check `view_map` WATER by the fields every day of a drought, and build the reservoir before the next one. After a drought, keep the pumps paused until the river near the fields has refilled.
- **Grow farms before the population (c8):** 96 kohlrabi plots and 48 bushes fed about 25 beavers. At 31 beavers the berries fell by about 70 a day, so I had to pause 2 pods. Rule of thumb: about 4 kohlrabi plots per beaver, and lay new fields as soon as the population passes about 3 beavers per 10 plots.
- **Forester areas cover future fields:** a Forester replants its whole planting area. Before turning woodland into a field, `clear_planting` the tree marks there, or the saplings come back. Lumberjacks ignore saplings, so to clear young trees fast use `demolish_resources` (builders do it).
- **Badwater doesn't water crops (Badtide 2, c8):** while the river ran contaminated, every field tile went dry (MOISTURE showed `w` only on water tiles) and nearly all crops died within 2 days: kohlrabi 96 -> 4, cassava and many bushes dead. Before a badtide: shut a Floodgate or Dam upstream of the fields so clean water stays beside them, and stock food for the badtide **plus about 3 days of regrowth** (replanted kohlrabi starts at 0%). After it ends, check that farmhouses replant at once and pause the pods.
- **Benchmark against a benchmark player's saves (12):** by cycle 4 their (Easy) colony had 11 Dams and 16 Levees, 4 deep pumps, 3 Inventors and 2 Scavengers; we had 4 Dams and 0 Levees by cycle 9. Do water engineering and science in the first cycles, not after the first crisis. Water wheels turn in badwater, so a badwater channel can still make power.
- **Water wheels: faster water = more power** (user). The in-game preview shows the power before placing. Put wheels where the flow is fastest (drops, narrow chokes, below dams/waterfalls), not just anywhere wet. Compare flow per spot before placing.
- **Dam where it holds the most water (user rule):** put the dam at the last choke before the creek drops off the terrace (most downstream point at the same bed height), so the whole creek upstream is held. One dam there beats several upstream (run 3: moved 3-tile dam at 103-105,144 to a 2-tile choke at x112 y153-154, the z14->z10 drop). Check the banks hold (bank height above bed) and give builders a path to an adjacent dry tile. A deep gorge below a drop (banks far above bed) is a future reservoir site for stacked levees.
- **Never build on the foot of a natural Slope** (run 3): a lumberjack flag at (70,154) sat at the bottom of the slope (69,154) and cut off the whole z16 pine forest; logs stalled for ~3 days with 3 lumberjacks. If logs stall while trees are marked, run `walk_distance` from the flag's doorstep to the trees' neighbour tiles; null = blocked.

## Run 3 post-mortem (ended c7d2 at the user's call; map-independent)
What happened: food was always just-in-time. A dip (c5d14), badtide 1 (c5d18) and then a 7-day drought (c6d15) each hit with almost no stock, so the colony went 57 -> 15 (30 starved, nearly all within one day once food had been 0 for ~3 days). Run total: +75 born, -76 died.
Root causes and the rule for next time:
- **Track food in days, not units.** days = edible stock / (3 x beavers). Target >= (longest hazard so far + 2) x 1.5 + 3 days of crop regrowth. Below 3 days = emergency, at any time of the cycle.
- **Pods are gated by food days.** I kept 5-7 pods running (population-first rule) while stock was near 0, so growth outran food. Run pods only while food days are above target; pause them before stock falls, not after.
- **Expect each hazard to be longer than the last** (drought went 5 -> 7 days). Plan for longest + 2.
- **Spread food across failure modes:** river-watered crops die in droughts and badtides; reservoir-watered crops survive if the reservoir is deep; indoor food (Hydroponic Garden) survives both. Have all three by cycle 4. I built the gardens during the famine, too late (8 days per batch).
- **Don't rely on water-wheel power for food.** Wheels stop in droughts, so the Food Factory stopped exactly when it was needed. Store rations ahead, or add stored power.
- **Spend science on hazard-proofing first.** I sat on 1100+ unspent science while food tech was missing.
- **Never silence the food alert for long.** Ignoring "food" in watch.py while "a known shortage plays out" let low food become normal.
- **Staff food first, every day.** Two farmhouses sat at 0 workers during the famine before I checked. When workers are short, farms, food workshops and pumps fill first.
- **Hazard checklist 3 days before every warning:** food days, water days, storage room for the reserve, crops near held water, pods vs food, power for food buildings.
- **Tools:** test every query on a quiet save before stream. One oversized view_map crashed the game live (fixed: the shim no longer uses LogException; the legend no longer overflows).
